# ML-2 — a risk model estimated from data, next to the mandate

Generated by `run_risk_model.py` on the real cached prices
(8 US large caps, 2514 trading days, 2015-01-05–2024-12-30).

`engine/risk.py` holds a **mandate**: caps per name, per sector, on
gross and net. Every one of those numbers is a declared input, so it
answers `is this position allowed?` and nothing else. `how much can
this book lose, and from what?` is a different question and it needs a
covariance matrix.

## 1. Two books the mandate cannot tell apart

| book | positions | gross | net | limits that bound |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A (spread across sectors) | JNJ, XOM, KO, JPM | 1.00 | +1.00 | none — both fully compliant |
| B (tech + index + a bank) | AAPL, MSFT, SPY, JPM | 1.00 | +1.00 | none — both fully compliant |

Same gross, same net, same position count, four names each, every
limit satisfied. **The mandate reports these as equivalent.**

## 2. What the covariance says about them

| | book A | book B |
|---|---|---|
| annualised volatility | 17.41% | 21.07% |
| parametric VaR 95% (daily) | 1.80% | 2.18% |
| parametric VaR 99% (daily) | 2.55% | 3.09% |
| diversification ratio | 1.307 | 1.193 |

Book B carries **21% more volatility** than book A at identical
gross. Both hold four names; in B, AAPL, MSFT and SPY are substantially
the same exposure wearing three tickers. The diversification ratio is
what says so — the closer to 1.000, the more the book is one position.

**There is no "effective number of bets" column here, and that is a
decision rather than an omission.** The standard entropy-over-principal-
components measure was implemented, measured, and rejected: it reads
1.004 at a pairwise correlation of 0.2, and every equity book shares a
market factor well above that, so it returns 1.0 for book A and 1.0 for
book B. `engine/risk_model.pca_effective_bets` keeps it next to the
tests that disqualified it. See §5.

## 3. Which position is driving the risk

| book | position | weight | risk share |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | JPM | 25% | 32.5% |
| A | XOM | 25% | 31.7% |
| A | KO | 25% | 18.4% |
| A | JNJ | 25% | 17.4% |
| B | AAPL | 25% | 28.4% |
| B | MSFT | 25% | 27.5% |
| B | JPM | 25% | 24.4% |
| B | SPY | 25% | 19.7% |

Every position is 25% of the book by weight and none of them is 25% of
the risk. That gap is the entire reason to decompose rather than sort
by size — **the largest position is almost never the largest risk**,
and a limits framework only ever sees the weight column.

## 4. Why the sample covariance is not good enough

With N assets you estimate N(N+1)/2 parameters from T observations.
Below is the same estimate on the real returns and then on a wide,
short panel of the kind a real 60-name book actually has.

| panel | assets | observations | parameters | condition number (sample) | (shrunk) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| real prices, full history | 8 | 2514 | 36 | 77 | 63 |
| real prices, last 40 days | 8 | 40 | 36 | 35 | 2 |
| 60 names, 80 days (T > N) | 60 | 80 | 1,830 | 167 | 1 |
| 60 names, 40 days (T < N) | 60 | 40 | 1,830 | 473,524,082,816,492 | 1 |

**The threshold is T < N, not T < N(N+1)/2, and this originally said
otherwise.** At 60 names and 80 days the sample matrix is badly
conditioned but still full rank — it inverts. At 60 names and 40 days
it is *rank deficient*: at most 39 non-zero eigenvalues for 60 assets,
so 21 directions carry exactly zero estimated variance. An optimiser
does not fail on that. It reads those directions as riskless and puts
the whole book there, which is why "minimum variance" portfolios come
back holding enormous offsetting positions in two nearly identical
names.

Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage pulls the estimate toward a constant-correlation
target at intensity **0.976** on the rank-deficient panel, and restores
full rank. The result is biased on purpose, because a biased matrix you
can invert beats an unbiased one you cannot.

## 5. A metric this rejected, and what rejected it

Meucci's *effective number of bets* — entropy of the variance spread
over principal components — is the natural answer to "how many
independent bets is this book?", and three implementations were tried
before none of them was reported.

| implementation | fails how |
|---|---|
| concentration of per-position risk contributions | an equal-weight book of three near-identical tech names has perfectly even contributions, so it scores well. On these prices it rated book B **above** book A — backwards. |
| entropy over principal components | exact on the *population* matrix (6.000 for six independent assets) and ~1.9 on a *sample* matrix of the same six with 4,000 observations. Near-equal eigenvalues make the eigenvectors an arbitrary rotation. |
| minimum-torsion (Löwdin) basis | fixes the independent case, then reports **two** effective bets for two perfectly correlated assets. Worse. |

The disqualifying number is the second one's hair trigger: at pairwise
correlation **0.2 it already reads 1.004**. Every equity book shares a
market factor well above that, so it answers 1.0 for a diversified book
and 1.0 for a concentrated one. A number that gives the same answer to
both questions is not a measurement, so what gets reported is the crude
diversification ratio, which has no entropy in it and does move in the
right direction on real data. The rejected version stays in
`engine/risk_model.pca_effective_bets` beside the tests that killed it,
so it does not get re-added by the next person to have the idea.

## 6. What this still does not buy

- **Every number here is an estimate from history**, and history is the
  one sample where those correlations held. Correlations rise in a
  crisis — exactly when the figure matters — so a covariance-based risk
  number is a statement about calm markets that gets quoted during
  storms. Book A's diversification is measured on a decade in which
  energy and staples happened to diverge.
- **The VaR is parametric**, a normal quantile on the portfolio vol. It
  understates the tail on real returns, which are fat-tailed. It is
  labelled parametric everywhere it appears because calling it plain
  "VaR" is how a risk report becomes reassuring.
- **Eight names is not a risk model's natural habitat.** The
  conditioning problem this section is about barely bites at N=8; the
  60-name row is there because that is where it does, and this repo's
  cached universe cannot reach it with real data.
- **No factor model.** Decomposing risk into market, size, value and
  momentum is what a real risk system reports, and it needs factor
  returns this repo does not have.
