# ML-2 — intraday execution, on a curve that was measured

Generated by `run_intraday.py` from 37,384 real 5-minute bars across
8 names, 2026-05-29 to 2026-08-24.

`engine/execution.py` states its own limit: *there is no intraday
scheduling ... the horizon is days and the unit is one day's volume.*
This is the intraday half. The first decision was **not to assume the
shape of the day** — assuming a U and then demonstrating consequences
of the assumption makes the conclusion a property of the assumption.

| | |

The last row is the compact statement: 78 buckets of clock time behave
like **43** buckets of liquidity. The day is roughly half as long
as it looks.

The profile is computed as a share WITHIN each ticker-day and then
averaged, not as total volume per bucket over total volume. Summing raw
volume would let the busiest name and the busiest days dominate, so the
curve would describe SPY on a heavy week rather than a typical day.

## 2. A stated participation rate is wrong at every interval

*"We traded at 5% of volume"* is a statement about the day. A TWAP
schedule — equal quantity per slice, the obvious default — holds the
QUANTITY constant while the volume under it swings 13-fold.

| stated rate | TWAP worst interval | overshoot | VWAP worst interval |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1% | 1.8% | **1.8x** | 1.0% |
| 5% | 9.2% | **1.8x** | 5.0% |
| 10% | 18.3% | **1.8x** | 10.0% |

A compliance limit written as *never exceed 10% of volume* is breached
by a TWAP schedule whose daily average is 5%. The aggregate number is
true and every interval disagrees with it.

## 3. What that costs

Impact is driven by participation, not by quantity, and the engine's
square-root law makes cost per slice go as `sqrt(participation)`. A
schedule that concentrates participation into the thinnest part of the
day pays more than its average rate suggests.

| stated rate | TWAP | VWAP | excess |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1% | 11.00 bp | 10.00 bp | **+10.0%** |
| 5% | 24.59 bp | 22.36 bp | **+10.0%** |
| 10% | 34.78 bp | 31.62 bp | **+10.0%** |
| 20% | 49.19 bp | 44.72 bp | **+10.0%** |

**The penalty is identical at every size — 10.0% — and that is a
check rather than a coincidence.** Under a square-root law the ratio
between two schedules is a pure function of the CURVE and cancels the
order size entirely. If it ever varied with size, the impact weighting
would be wrong. A test pins it.

## 4. A claim this refuted about its own design

The textbook cost of VWAP is that it **defers** execution: tracking the
volume curve pushes the order into the close, leaving the remainder
exposed to timing risk for longer — the same trade `execution.py`
prices over days. This document asserted that before measuring it.

| | TWAP | VWAP |
|---|---|---|
| timing exposure (avg fraction outstanding) | 0.4936 | **0.4840** |
| complete by 12:00 | 39.7% | **46.1%** |
| complete by 15:00 | 85.9% | 75.6% |

**VWAP is slightly *less* deferred, not more.** A U is not a ramp: the
textbook trade-off assumes a back-loaded curve, and this one is
front-loaded too. The open auction at 6.96% roughly offsets the close
at 8.78%, so following the curve starts the order sooner as well as
finishing it later. On this profile VWAP is better on impact and no
worse on timing, and the trade-off simply is not there.

Where the trade-off *is* real is alpha. Neither schedule knows anything
about the next few hours; a signal that decays within the day is a
reason to trade faster than either, and the timing-exposure number
cannot see it because it prices dispersion rather than drift.

## 5. The closing auction is not continuous trading

| | share of the order landing in an auction bucket |
|---|---|
| TWAP | 2.56% |
| VWAP | 15.75% |

The 15:55 bucket is 8.78% of the day and is mostly the closing
auction — a single price formed once, not continuous flow. *Submitting*
5% of the auction is a different act from *participating* at 5%: you
send an order in and find out what it printed at. Treating that bucket
as more of the same overstates how much of a schedule can actually be
placed there, which is why the share is flagged rather than folded into
the impact number.

It also means VWAP's advantage is partly an accounting one. It earns
its lower impact by putting **16%** of the order into buckets it
cannot participate in the ordinary way.

## What this still does not model

- **60 days, 8 large caps.** Enough to measure the SHAPE, which is
  stable and well documented. Not enough to say anything about a
  particular day, an earnings date, an index rebalance, or a name that
  does not trade like these.
- **The curve is a forecast used as if it were known.** A real VWAP
  algo predicts today's volume and is wrong; the error is itself a cost
  and it is not modelled here.
- **No limit orders, no venue routing, no adverse selection.** Every
  slice is assumed to execute at the participation it asked for. A real
  schedule leaks information, and the fills that arrive most easily are
  the ones you least want.
- **No spread.** Impact here is the square-root term only. Crossing the
  spread on every slice is a separate cost that TWAP and VWAP pay
  differently, because they cross a different number of times.
